# Orivory — Market Research: AI Second Brain, Self-Hosted PKM & Open-Source Monetization

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Method:** Desk research via Exa web search + page fetches (20+ distinct queries). Every non-obvious claim carries a source URL. GitHub star counts were read from GitHub pages/APIs surfaced in search results during the same window (early Sep 2026) and drift over time. Market-report numbers from aggregator firms (Precedence, Market Intelo, Dataintelo, The Business Research Company, Technavio) are treated as **directionally useful but low-confidence**: they are paywalled reports with inconsistent, sometimes contradictory figures, and their methodology is not auditable. Where credible (self-reported revenue, GitHub, funding PRs, community surveys), the source is labeled.

**Product context assumed:** Orivory = open-source (MIT), self-hosted "AI second brain": FastAPI + Next.js, LangGraph multi-agent RAG (15+ agents, corrective RAG), hybrid vector+keyword search with reranking, personal memory store (salience/decay), knowledge graph (entity extraction, clusters), proactive insights/digest, connectors (web clipper + RSS production-grade; Notion/Gmail/Drive immature), team workspaces, analytics, referral system. MVP, pre-launch, no users, no pricing.

### (c) Self-hosted / homelab trend
This is the most credible "market" evidence for Orivory's wedge, because it's community-measured, not consultancy-modeled:
- **r/selfhosted ≈ 790K–821K members, growing ~+42.5%/yr (+245K members in the past year)** ([Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-selfhosted/), [DreamHost blog](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/self-hosting/)).
- **selfh.st 2025 user survey: 4,081 responses**; >80% run Linux, Docker dominant ([selfh.st survey results](https://selfh.st/survey/2025-results/), [It's FOSS analysis](https://itsfoss.com/opinion/self-hosting-rising/)). 2024 survey got ~3,700 responses, nearly double 2023 ([Ars Technica interview with selfh.st founder](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/self-hosting-is-having-a-moment-ethan-sholly-knows-why/)). Drivers: privacy, cloud cost, subscription fatigue, easy Docker installs.
- Only **40% of self-hosters donated to any project in the past year** despite heavy usage (2024 survey, Ars Technica above) — donations are not a monetization strategy; ~**60% said "no" to donating**.
- Deployyn's 2025 community survey (850+ respondents) lists **Karakeep and Open WebUI among the apps newly adopted in 2025** — self-hosted AI/knowledge tools are crossing into the mainstream homelab stack ([selfhosted-survey-2025.deployn.de/new](https://selfhosted-survey-2025.deployn.de/new/)).
- "State of Self-Hosting 2026" analysis of 1,165 articles across 370 apps: **Note-taking & Knowledge is the #2 most-covered category** (after media servers), and 6 of the 12 most resource-hungry self-hosted apps are AI/ML tools — a "two-tier" self-hosting world ([selfhosting.sh](https://selfhosting.sh/research/state-of-self-hosting-2026/)).
- A CISPA research figure cited by DreamHost: ~**8.5% of the U.S. population self-hosts at least one internet-facing service** ([DreamHost](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/self-hosting/)). A "$15.6B → $85.2B by 2034 self-hosting market" figure appears in the same post but has no traceable methodology — treat as marketing.
- **Piracy/media is the #1 use case, knowledge is #2** — the self-host audience skews technical, privacy-motivated, and price-averse ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/self-hosting-is-having-a-moment-ethan-sholly-knows-why/)).

**Competitive reading:** Orivory's *feature list* (multi-agent RAG + hybrid retrieval + memory + KG + proactive digest + connectors + teams) exists across **six different competitors, none of which has all of it in one open-source self-hosted app**: Onyx has the retrieval stack but is enterprise-search-shaped; Khoj had the breadth but killed its cloud and its future is uncertain; AnythingLLM has teams+agents but no memory/KG/digest; Karakeep has capture+AI tagging but no brain; Mem0/Zep/Cognee have memory/KG but are dev-infra without an app; Smart Connections/Saner.ai have proactive surfacing but are closed or plugin-bound. The risk is the reverse: every one of these ships faster with more hands on deck.

## 3. White Space Analysis

Verdicts: **CROWDED** / **OPEN** / **OPEN-BUT-HARD**.

### (a) Privacy-first self-hosted with best-in-class retrieval quality + citations — **OPEN-BUT-HARD (recommended)**
- Who's there: Onyx explicitly markets "hybrid-search, advanced RAG, contextual retrieval, LLM-based knowledge graphs" and runs blind LLM-judged evals over 220K documents ([onyx.app](https://onyx.app/)); Khoj, AnythingLLM, Open WebUI all do document RAG; Obsidian Copilot's large-vault indexing failures and Smart Connections' "RAG too weak" complaints ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/logancyang/obsidian-copilot/issues/1943), [forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/alternatives-to-smart-connections/108886)) show retrieval quality at personal scale is *unsolved*.
- Open because: nobody in the *personal* self-hosted tier treats retrieval quality as a measurable, benchmarked product feature — that's enterprise-search language. Orivory's corrective-RAG + reranking + citations maps directly to the same complaint pattern (wrong/missing answers) that made Smart Connections users defect.
- Hard because: Onyx could pivot down-market; Khoj's 36.8K-star community is orphaned by the cloud shutdown and would be receptive to a credible successor — you must catch them with clear proof of quality.

### (b) Proactive surfacing (digest/insights) instead of reactive chat — **OPEN (clearest differentiator)**
- Who's there: **Saner.ai** is the only product reviewed whose headline is proactive AI ("Skai plans your day") and it's closed, tiny, and criticized as beta ([Litmus](https://litmustools.com/review/saner-ai/), [founder-facing user quote on saner.ai](https://www.saner.ai/): "Saner is the only AI-based tool that truly feels like a personal assistant"). Mem's "Custom Routines" (get.mem.ai/pricing) price them behind $29+/mo caps. Khoj had scheduled automations but its cloud died; no open-source self-hosted competitor leads with a daily digest. Tana/Mem market "AI that anticipates", NotebookLM is reactive. The Business Research Company lists "autonomous systems for intelligent information management" as a 2026 market trend ([TBRC](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/personal-knowledge-base-artificial-intelligence-ai-global-market-report)).
- Open because: "inbox for your mind" / scheduled digest over YOUR data, self-hosted, is unclaimed. It's also the natural monetization surface (email digests, proactive agents = ongoing compute → subscription logic).

### (c) Knowledge-graph visualization as first-class UI — **OPEN-BUT-HARD**
- Who's there: Zep Graphiti (30.5K stars) and Cognee (30.4K stars) prove developer appetite for entity/relationship memory but ship **no end-user graph UI** (Graphiti's dashboard is cloud-only debugging; [cognee.ai](https://www.cognee.ai/)). Obsidian has a static link-graph view users love but no AI entity extraction; Trilium has knowledge-graph tags without AI clustering; Tana sells structure without AI graphs.
- Open because: an auto-built, *visualized* entity graph ("your second brain you can see") over self-hosted personal data doesn't exist as a product.
- Hard because: graph UIs are famously demo-appealing but retention-weak (most Obsidian graph views are looked at, not used); entity extraction quality on personal corpora is hard; Graphiti/Cognee could ship UI quickly given funding.

### (d) Team/workspace tier for small teams (open-core monetization) — **CROWDED (as a wedge), VIABLE (as a tier)**
- Who's there and winning: AnythingLLM ($50–99/mo cloud, free multi-user self-host), Onyx ($20/user/mo cloud + EE license), Open WebUI (enterprise licensing), Karakeep cloud, plus every SaaS competitor. Team features are the *default* monetization path — meaning differentiation must come from elsewhere, and free multi-user self-host is already table stakes ([anythingllm.com/cloud](https://anythingllm.com/cloud), [teamazing Onyx review](https://www.teamazing.com/blog/onyx-ai-enterprise-review-2026/)).
- Verdict: don't lead with "teams"; ship it as tier 2 behind (a)+(b). Note the cautionary tale: Khoj's cloud-first team strategy collapsed ([app.khoj.dev](https://app.khoj.dev/)).

### (e) Connector niches: email/RSS-heavy users, researchers — **OPEN**
- Who's there: Karakeep (bookmarks/clipper, no RSS), Memos (no AI), Khoj (Notion/GitHub files), Onyx (Slack/Drive/Confluence but enterprise-permissioned), Mem (Gmail, closed), Saner.ai (Gmail/Calendar, closed). **No open-source second brain pairs a production-grade RSS reader + web clipper + Gmail with the RAG/memory stack.** Meanwhile self-hosted RSS (FreshRSS) is a top-20 homelab staple ([selfh.st survey](https://selfh.st/survey/2025-results/); It's FOSS lists "knowledge (FreshRSS, Paperless-ngx)" as a core stack) and Pocket's shutdown (Mozilla) created a migration wave Karakeep already rode ([Karakeep README](https://github.com/karakeep-app/karakeep)).
- Researchers: NotebookLM's 110M visits show the demand; nothing self-hosted serves it.

### (f) Regional/language niche (Vietnamese/SEA) — **OPEN-BUT-HARD (real but second-order)**
- Signals: Vietnam's draft national AI strategy targets 5 Vietnamese-language models, 500K AI workers, AI ≈6% of GDP by 2030 ([Vietnam News via digitalinasia](https://digitalinasia.com/vietnam-ai-market/)); Viettel built a 120B-parameter Vietnamese LLM; **ChatGPT Go launched in Vietnam at ~US$5/mo (Oct 2025)** — cloud AI is now aggressively cheap there; an Apache-2.0 Vietnamese RAG toolkit (Nôm) already exists with hybrid retrieval + reranking + citations ([nrl.ai/nom](https://www.nrl.ai/en/nom)).
- Verdict: language is not a moat (Vietnamese models/embeddings are being commoditized by state-backed players), but it can be a *distribution* wedge (r/selfhosted has strong SEA/India/Japan participation; MCP search volume shows Japan/India at #2/#3 globally per [Koenig analysis](https://academy.kspl.tech/blog/2026-05-31-mcp-server-adoption-2026)). Only worth doing after English-language PMF.

## 4. Monetization Patterns for Open-Source AI Tools

What actually works, with numbers:

1. **Open-source core + paid hosted cloud (the "Plausible pattern").** Plausible (AGPL, 100% open, fully self-hostable) crossed **$1M ARR in June 2022** and is estimated at **~$3.1M ARR (2024)** with 4–10 people and zero ad spend — proving people pay *to not run it*, not for features ([Plausible blog](https://plausible.io/blog/open-source-saas), [GetLatka estimate](https://getlatka.com/companies/plausible-analytics), [OperatorBook analysis](https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/plausible-analytics-revenue-1m-arr-against-google)). Key insight: self-hosted users generated ~0% of revenue but ~all of the trust/top-of-funnel.
2. **Non-profit + managed hosting at scale (Ghost).** Ghost publishes finances live: **$10.88M ARR, ~907K MRR, 30,441 customers, zero VC** ([ghost.org via OperatorBook](https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/ghost-revenue-10m-arr-non-profit-in-public)). Managed hosting is the entire business; self-host is free forever.
3. **Privacy-adjacent bootstrap (Fathom Analytics).** Never published revenue; third-party estimate **$5–10M ARR**, bootstrapped, acquired Dec 2024 ([OperatorBook](https://www.operatorbook.dev/stories/fathom-analytics-revenue-the-number-they-wont-publish)). Privacy-first markets support real businesses even against free defaults.
4. **Open-core with `ee/` directory + cloud (Onyx, Dokploy, GitLab pattern).** Onyx: MIT CE + paid EE (SSO, permission sync, analytics) + $20/user cloud; raised **$10M seed (Mar 2025)** ([teamazing](https://www.teamazing.com/blog/onyx-ai-enterprise-review-2026/)). Dokploy moved to **Apache-2.0 core + source-available `proprietary/` for future enterprise features, with an explicit "never move open features to paid" pledge** — and reached **36.7K stars in ~2 years** ([Dokploy licensing discussion](https://github.com/Dokploy/dokploy/discussions/3), [repo](https://github.com/dokploy/dokploy)).
5. **Freemium SaaS with metered AI (closed-source benchmarks).** Granola free→$14→$35/user; Mem $0→$199/mo with usage caps; Tana AI credits; Notion agents at $10/1K credits. Note Granola's Feb 2026 free-tier squeeze backlash ([recatools](https://recatools.com/ai-directory/granola/)) — caps on *personal notes* generate disproportionate anger.
6. **Usage-based LLM margin.** Memory/infra vendors price by credits/operations: Mem0 free/$19/$79/$249 ([theaiagentindex](https://theaiagentindex.com/agents/mem0)); Zep $125→$1,250/yr Flex tiers ([getzep.com/pricing](https://www.getzep.com/pricing/)); AnythingLLM explicitly "bring your own LLM key" for cloud and skips margin entirely. For Orivory: digest/proactive agents consume ongoing compute → this is the natural place to charge, either as hosted margin or a "bring your own key + pay for hosting" split.
7. **Sponsorships/donations: NOT viable as a strategy.** Only ~40% of self-hosters donated to *any* project in a year ([Ars Technica/selfh.st](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/self-hosting-is-having-a-moment-ethan-sholly-knows-why/)); Dokploy's sponsors page shows a handful of $5–100/mo sponsors for a 36K-star project ([GitHub Sponsors](https://github.com/sponsors/Dokploy), [Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/dokploy)); Plausible's early donations totaled **six $5 donations** ([Plausible blog](https://plausible.io/blog/open-source-saas)). Fine as a gesture, not as payroll.
8. **Paid plugin/pro tier inside an ecosystem.** Obsidian's Smart Connections (1.1M downloads) moved advanced AI into Pro (~$20/mo) to fund maintenance — and suffered visible community backlash ([forum thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/alternatives-to-smart-connections/108886)). Open WebUI added a branding clause at >50 users to protect enterprise licensing ([license](https://docs.openwebui.com/license/)).
9. **Negative case — Khoj:** YC-backed, 36.8K stars, cloud-first subscription + six clients → **cloud service shut down April 2026** with an honest postmortem: "initial bets… made it very difficult to scale in utility" ([app.khoj.dev](https://app.khoj.dev/), [blog](https://blog.khoj.dev/posts/10k-stars-in/)). Lessons: one excellent client beats six; self-host-first is fine but the *paid* thing must be something self-hosters can't trivially replicate.

**Best-fit pattern for Orivory (MIT-licensed):** MIT is maximally forkable, so monetization should ride things forks can't take: the hosted experience (convenience), proactive digests (ongoing compute + notifications infra), team sync/admin, and future enterprise SSO/RBAC. Dokploy-style "Apache/MIT core stays free forever, `proprietary/` for enterprise" pledge is cheap goodwill that protects the community story. Given Mem0/Zep/Graphiti momentum, an API-first surface for Orivory's memory store (MCP server) is also a plausible future paid/hosted hook — every serious OSS AI tool now ships MCP (Khoj, AnythingLLM, Trilium, Graphiti, Cognee all do).

## 5. Distribution: How OSS AI Knowledge Tools Actually Get Adoption

Documented trajectories:

- **Hacker News Show HN is the classic ignition, and the *second* attempt usually works.** Khoj's first front-page hit ("AI personal assistant for your digital brain", July 2023) "got roasted for being mere chatgpt wrappers and not being open source enough"; the follow-up Show HN ("Chat offline with your second brain using Llama 2") scored **565 points, 150 comments** and reset the narrative ([HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36933452), [Khoj postmortem blog](https://blog.khoj.dev/posts/10k-stars-in/)). Reor's Show HN hit 411 points (Feb 2024) ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39372159)). Plausible treated *every revenue milestone* as a fresh HN post ($500K ARR, $1M ARR, $1.2M ARR — the $1M post took 522 points), making HN a recurring channel, not a one-shot ([Systemaic teardown](https://www.systemaic.com/teardowns/plausible)).
- **GitHub Trending + YouTube/Twitter are the growth multiplier.** Khoj's 10K-star moment: "Some delightful YouTube/Twitter posts made us go trending at #1 on Github. We scrambled to handle the big traffic spike" ([blog.khoj.dev](https://blog.khoj.dev/posts/10k-stars-in/)).
- **r/selfhosted is where capture/knowledge tools compound.** Karakeep's author is a self-hoster who built it for his own Pocket-replacement need, announced the Pocket-shutdown-driven rename on r/selfhosted, and the app was voted a "new app adopted in 2025" in the Deployyn community survey ([README origin story](https://github.com/karakeep-app/karakeep), [rebrand post](https://leminal.space/post/17840191/14730626), [Deployyn survey](https://selfhosted-survey-2025.deployn.de/new/)). r/selfhosted's ~821K members growing 42%/yr is the single best free channel for Orivory's wedge ([Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-selfhosted/)).
- **awesome-selfhosted + selfh.st directories** are the standing discovery layer; "Note Taking & Knowledge" is the #2 covered category in ecosystem write-ups ([selfhosting.sh](https://selfhosting.sh/research/state-of-self-hosting-2026/)).
- **1-click deploy templates** (Docker Hub, Elestio, Railway, community NAS guides) lower the funnel: AnythingLLM maintains deploy buttons for 10+ platforms ([README](https://github.com/Mintplex-Labs/anything-llm)); QNAP and NAS vendors now market Ollama+Open WebUI stacks directly ([QNAP guide](https://www.qnap.com/it-it/reference/stop-ai-data-leaks:-run-private-llms-locally-on-a-nas)).
- **Product Hunt + YC Launch** for the mainstream layer: AnythingLLM launched on PH and is listed as a YC company ([YC profile](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/anythingllm)).
- **MCP servers as a new distribution channel (2025–2026):** 10K+ public MCP servers, 97M+ monthly SDK downloads, clients incl. ChatGPT/Cursor/Gemini/Copilot/VS Code ([Anthropic donation announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-the-model-context-protocol-and-establishing-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation), [AgentsCamp stats](https://agentscamp.com/guides/mcp/mcp-ecosystem-statistics)). A well-made "Orivory memory" MCP server would put Orivory inside Claude/ChatGPT sessions — Khoj, AnythingLLM, Trilium, Graphiti, Cognee all already ship one.
- **Timing hooks beat generic launches:** Pocket's death (Mozilla) was a migration wave for Karakeep; Plex pricing changes and Redis licensing flips produce similar waves ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2025/05/self-hosting-is-having-a-moment-ethan-sholly-knows-why/), [selfhosting.sh](https://selfhosting.sh/research/state-of-self-hosting-2026/)). Orivory's analogs: Khoj Cloud's shutdown (orphaned self-hosters), Reor's archival, Smart Connections' paywall churn.

**Playbook implied by the evidence:** (1) ship a Docker one-liner + Obsidian/markdown import first, (2) Show HN once the digest/quality story is demoable, (3) seed r/selfhosted with the build-in-public story, (4) get listed in awesome-selfhosted/selfh.st, (5) publish an MCP server, (6) re-launch at every milestone (stars, digest quality benchmarks) the way Plausible did.

**Rising:**
1. **Agentic memory as a funded category.** Mem0: $24M raise, 64.5K stars, 186M API calls in Q3 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/mem0-raises-24m-from-yc-peak-xv-and-basis-set-to-build-the-memory-layer-for-ai-apps/)); Cognee $7.5M seed ([Menlo Times](https://www.menlotimes.com/post/cognee-is-building-memory-for-ai-agents)); Letta $10M; Graphiti 30K stars. Orivory's salience/decay memory store is riding the same wave — expect users to benchmark it against Mem0.

BYO-LLM support (Ollama/OpenAI-compatible) is now assumed, not a feature.

5. **Proactive/agentic "assistant that acts" positioning.** Market trend lists now name "autonomous systems for intelligent information management" and "agentic workflows" as the 2026–2030 trend ([TBRC](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/personal-knowledge-base-artificial-intelligence-ai-global-market-report)); Mem routines, Saner proactive AI, Notion AI agents all ship forms of it.
6. **AI browsers/clipper layer.** Perplexity Comet, Dia, Gemini-in-Chrome and a reported OpenAI browser are reshaping where capture happens ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/ai-powered-web-browsers-want-help-people-time-are-effective-rcna218039)); clipper-plus-AI products proliferate (ClippedNote, Qind, SyncNos — all capturing AI chats/articles into searchable stores). Capture is being commoditized; *the brain behind the clipper* is the differentiator.

**Saturating / declining:**
1. **Generic "chat with your documents" self-hosted RAG.** Open WebUI (150K stars), AnythingLLM (65K), LibreChat and friends have made doc-chat a solved commodity; several competing reviews now treat it as a category, not a differentiator ([doolpa AnythingLLM review](https://doolpa.com/article/anythingllm)).
2. **Agent frameworks as products** — consolidating (Letta folded bets into Letta Code; the value moved to memory/state).
3. **Pure bookmark managers** — Karakeep/Linkwarden/Linkding are abundant; Karakeep only won by adding AI tagging.
4. **Meeting-note AI** — the most crowded AI sub-market (30+ players listed by Global Info Research, incl. Otter, Fireflies, Granola, Fathom; [GlobalInfoResearch](https://www.globalinforesearch.com/reports/2641190/ai-notetaker)). Orivory should not drift here.
5. **Donation-supported OSS** — demonstrated non-viable (selfh.st survey; Plausible's six $5 donations).

## 7. Key Takeaways for Orivory

1. **The market dollars are mush; the community signals are solid.** Ignore the $X-billion AI-notes reports; anchor on r/selfhosted (+42%/yr, ~821K), selfh.st surveys, and star velocity of Karakeep/AnythingLLM/Onyx. The self-hosted knowledge category is real and growing.
2. **The "AI second brain" slot in the self-hosted world is genuinely vacant right now:** Khoj Cloud is dead (Apr 2026), Reor is archived, Smart Connections alienated users with its paywall, Logseq is stalled. There is an orphaned audience of privacy-first knowledge-hoarders with no obvious new home.
3. **No single OSS competitor combines Orivory's stack.** Onyx = retrieval depth without personal capture; AnythingLLM = teams+agents without memory/KG/digest; Karakeep = capture without brain; Mem0/Zep/Cognee = memory/KG infra without an app. The synthesis is the product.
4. **Lead with proactive digest, not chat.** Reactive doc-chat is saturated (Open WebUI/AnythingLLM). Saner.ai is the only proactive-first player and it's closed/beta/tiny. "Your brain emails you every morning" is unclaimed in open source and matches the 2026 agentic trend.
5. **Make retrieval quality measurable and public.** Users defect from Obsidian Copilot/Smart Connections over weak RAG and index corruption; Onyx wins enterprise mindshare by publishing blind evals. Orivory's corrective RAG + hybrid search + citations should ship with a public benchmark page.
6. **Treat the knowledge graph as a product surface, not an infra detail.** Graphiti/Cognee prove demand for KG memory but ship no end-user visualization; Obsidian's beloved-but-dumb link graph shows users want *to see* their brain. An auto-extracted, visualized entity graph is demo-gold and retention-risky — prototype retention before over-investing.
7. **Monetize the hosted experience + ongoing compute, never the notes.** Plausible ($3.1M est.), Ghost ($10.8M live ARR), Fathom ($5–10M est.) all sell "we run it so you don't" against free self-host. Proactive digests/agents = recurring compute = the natural paid unit. Put SSO/RBAC/audit into a future `proprietary/` or cloud-only tier (Onyx/Dokploy pattern) and pledge publicly that core features never move to paid.
8. **MIT is fine but invites forks — moat = pace + hosted quality + MCP surface.** Ship an Orivory MCP server early: 97M monthly SDK downloads and 10K+ servers make MCP the cheapest distribution channel into Claude/ChatGPT/Cursor sessions.
9. **Distribution playbook is well-templated:** Docker one-liner → Show HN (expect a rough first pass; Khoj's second Show HN did 565 points) → r/selfhosted build-in-public → awesome-selfhosted/selfh.st listings → GitHub Trending via YouTube/Twitter coverage → re-launch at milestones.
10. **Teams = tier 2, not the wedge.** Free multi-user self-host is table stakes (AnythingLLM); Onyx owns the paid team search lane at $20/user. Teams make sense only after the personal product has gravity — and Khoj's collapse is the cautionary tale for cloud-first team bets.
11. **Connector niche = RSS/email is a real wedge.** FreshRSS is a top-tier homelab staple and Pocket's death created migration waves; no OSS brain pairs production RSS+clipper+Gmail with RAG+memory. "Researcher/reader second brain" is the sharpest initial ICP.
12. **Watch the memory-infra incumbents as frenemies.** Mem0 ($24M), Zep, Cognee set user expectations and could ship an app layer; their pricing moves (Zep's 10x entry-price jump) also push small users toward self-hosted alternatives — timing Orivory's launch to those churn moments is free distribution.
